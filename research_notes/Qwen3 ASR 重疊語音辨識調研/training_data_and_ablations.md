# Training data, simulation recipes and training configurations for single-channel multi-talker ASR (with emphasis on fine-tuning pretrained ASR / Speech-LLM models)

Evidence labels used throughout:
- **[P]** read in the primary source (paper PDF text, official repo file, or model card) during this research.
- **[S]** seen only in a search-result snippet or abstract; not checked against the full text.
- **[R]** number reconstructed from a flattened PDF table; row/column mapping is my reading and should be re-checked before quoting.

## Per-paper fact table (initial model, data, modules trained, single-speaker check, ablations)

### Takeaway
Across 25+ systems, the recurring recipe is: start from a strong single-talker model, train on on-the-fly simulated partially-overlapped mixtures that keep a large share of single-speaker samples, then fine-tune on (or mix in) real conversational data. Synthetic-only models work on matched synthetic tests but lose badly on real far-field meetings; no paper found reports a Qwen3-ASR or Taigi multi-talker fine-tune.

#### C. MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize specifics (asked explicitly)
- Target format is `[start_time][Sxx]text[end_time]`, seconds, segments concatenated into one stream, e.g. `[0.48][S01]Welcome everyone[1.66][12.26][S02]...` — [MOSS repo README](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize) [P]
- Weights (0.9B) were open-sourced on 2026-07-09; a fine-tuning script exists: `finetune.py` on the HF `Trainer`, JSONL of (text prompt, audio path, reference transcript), example flags `--learning_rate 1e-5 --num_train_epochs 3 --per_device_train_batch_size 1 --bf16 --gradient_checkpointing`, default max sequence length 131,072 tokens. So fine-tuning code is public; the original training pipeline, data and simulator are not in the repo — [FINETUNING.md](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize/blob/main/FINETUNING.md) [P]
- Simulation recipe in the report: draw 2-12 distinct speakers, one utterance each; split each utterance into contiguous word runs (segment count sampled, log-normal weights); place on one timeline with Gaussian inter-segment gaps, enforce speaker alternation, allow overlaps capped at 80% of the shorter segment; add real noise and reverberation with SNR uniform in 0-15 dB — [MOSS report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01554) [P]
- Reported results: AISHELL-4 CER 14.84% / cpCER 15.83% [S]; DM-ASR's table lists MOSS at cpCER 15.82 (AliMeeting) and 15.83 (AISHELL-4) [P, via [DM-ASR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22467)].
- A secondary summary site gave specific hour counts (AISHELL-4 about 150 h, podcasts about 200 h, about 100 h simulated, 3:1 real:simulated). These numbers do **not** appear in the report text I read and conflict with DM-ASR's statement that "its total training hours are not reported"; treat them as unreliable — [Emergent Mind page, via search](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/moss-transcribe-diarize) [S]

### Inferences
- The two closest architectural analogues to "Qwen3-ASR-0.6B + LoRA" in the literature are DM-ASR (Qwen3-0.6B decoder, and separately a Qwen3-ASR-1.7B encoder, both LoRA r=16) and MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize (Qwen3-0.6B-style decoder). Neither is a pure SOT system without diarization/timestamps, and neither trained on synthetic-only data.
- Every Speech-LLM paper that reports good overlapped results either unfreezes/adapts the encoder at some stage or uses an encoder already exposed to overlap (WavLM). No evidence was found that LLM-side LoRA alone on a single-talker-trained encoder is sufficient.

### Gaps
- Chan & Lian full text (scaling curve, synthesis parameters, Whisper-size table, single-speaker baseline) could not be read: sciencedirect.com and ssrn.com were unreachable. Only the abstract and the official repo were used.
- VarArray + t-SOT hours/config, USTC ablations, SE-DiCoW results, Cornell et al. 2024 (TTS+LLM synthetic conversations, [arXiv 2408.09215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09215)) and Nguyen & Waibel 2024 ("Synthetic conversations improve multi-talker ASR", ICASSP 2024) were not extracted.
- No published multi-talker fine-tune of Qwen3-ASR itself was found in this scope.

## Simulation recipes and their parameters

### Takeaway
The dominant ASR recipe is very simple: sum 1-N utterances at original (or lightly randomised) volume with a random start delay that guarantees partial overlap, generated on the fly so mixtures never repeat. Loudness normalisation, clipping control, noise and RIR appear mainly in separation/diarization-oriented simulators (LibriMix, NeMo, FastMSS), and one study finds noise/reverb matters little for a Whisper-based ASR model.

- t-SOT cites 6-15% of speaking time being overlapped in meetings — [t-SOT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00842) [P]
- In "Mind the Gap", for DiCoW "augmentation has a modest effect overall": noise moves macro tcpWER 14.0 to 13.7, reverb alone does not help, noise+reverb 13.8; the authors attribute this to Whisper's large-scale pre-training. - Yang et al. found that short-segmenting source utterances at silences "introduced an unnatural onset / offset of the audio", which they suspect caused the single-speaker-region degradation — [Yang et al. 2023](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/ICASSP2023_Real_Simulation_tSOT.pdf) [P]

### Inferences
- For an SOT-style ASR fine-tune (no diarization output), the evidence favours the Kanda-style on-the-fly partial-overlap mix over LibriMix-style full overlap: DiCoW and SE-DiCoW authors both call fully-overlapped Libri2Mix unrealistic, and Mind the Gap shows boosted (but still conversational) overlap is what helps ASR.
- None of the ASR-oriented recipes that keep "original volume" address clipping; only LibriMix does. With heterogeneous Taigi/Mandarin/English corpora recorded at different levels, an explicit loudness step (LibriMix-style LUFS range) is a reasonable borrowing, but this is not something any reviewed ASR paper ablated.

### Gaps
- No paper reviewed ablates SIR range for SOT-style ASR. SIR choices are stated, not justified.
- Lhotse simulator level handling and the jsalt2020-simulate configs were not examined in detail.
- LibriCSS construction parameters (0S/0L/10-40% overlap, replayed in a real room) were not re-verified beyond the t-SOT result table.

## Synthetic-vs-real gap, benefit of real fine-tuning, and cases where synthetic data hurt

### Takeaway
Simulation-only training leaves a large gap on real far-field meetings (roughly 2x WER in two independent studies), a few tens of hours of real data close most of it, and the best results come from mixing or sequencing synthetic then real. Synthetic data did hurt in specific cases: too high a synthetic share, a mismatched simulation style, and single-speaker regions.

### Cited Findings
- Kanda 2021, AMI-SDM eval WER: SOT model pre-trained on 75K h simulation only **44.2%**; trained on AMI only **59.1%**; pre-trained then fine-tuned on AMI **21.2%**. Speaker counting for 3-speaker groups was 95.6% "2 speakers" before fine-tuning — [Kanda 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16776) [P]
- Same paper, what the real data contributes (fine-tuning data ablation, dev/eval): none 41.6 / 44.2; AMI-IHM randomly mixed (in-domain language/accent, random overlap) 25.0 / 29.0; AMI-IHM real-mix (real overlap pattern, close-talk) 19.6 / 23.0; AMI-SDM (real noise/reverb too) 18.4 / 21.2. Authors: the largest gain comes from "LM, English accent, and the accurate overlap of speech region", the second from "the real overlapping pattern", and noise/reverb is "relatively small" — [Kanda 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16776) [P]
- Yang et al. 2023, t-SOT TT18, fine-tune mixture of real (AMI+ICSI) : simulated, AMI dev / AMI eval / ICSI dev / ICSI eval / internal meetings: 1.0:0 gives 21.9 / 25.7 / 20.3 / 17.9 / 24.9; 0.5:0.5 gives 21.6 / 25.4 / 19.5 / 17.1 / 24.6; 0.2:0.8 gives 21.8 / 25.5 / 19.9 / 17.3 / 23.4; **0.1:0.9 gives 22.4 / 26.1 / 21.0 / 18.2 / 23.1; 0:1.0 gives 30.0 / 35.3 / 28.7 / 25.4 / 24.0**. So simulation-only is about 10 points worse on AMI, and pushing the simulated share from 80% to 90% made AMI and ICSI worse while the out-of-domain internal set kept improving — [Yang et al. 2023](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/ICASSP2023_Real_Simulation_tSOT.pdf) [P]
- Same paper: real-only fine-tuning had to be cut to 2.5K steps "to avoid over-fitting", versus 20K steps when simulation was included [P]
- Same paper, simulation quality: random mixing K=2 35.9 AMI dev; after filtering source audio containing background speakers 31.6; K=5 31.2; best blend of random : word-based : time-based at 0.3 : 0.3 : 0.4 gives 30.0 [P]
- SURT (icefall), AMI WER simulated-only then after adaptation on AMI+ICSI: SURT-base IHM-Mix 39.8 to 37.4, **SDM 65.4 to 46.9**, MDM 46.6 to 43.7; SURT-large SDM 62.5 to 44.6. ICSI SDM 60.0 to 33.9 (base) — [icefall AMI SURT README](https://github.com/k2-fsa/icefall/blob/master/egs/ami/SURT/README.md) [P]
- Mind the Gap, DiCoW tcpWER (NOTSOFAR-1 / AMI-SDM / LibriSpeechMix 1,2,3-spk / Mixer6 / macro): synthetic only 20.1 / 16.0 / 1.8, 2.4, 4.0 / 14.7 / **9.8**; real only 17.7 / 15.5 / 2.8, 5.9, 10.5 / 12.9 / **10.9**; real + synthetic 16.3 / 15.2 / 1.9, 2.5, 4.1 / 12.7 / **8.8**; synthetic then real 16.3 / 14.9 / 1.9, 2.5, 3.9 / 12.4 / **8.7** — [Mind the Gap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15442) [P]
- Same paper, source domain matters more than quantity: LibriSpeech-sourced synthetic data reaches 1.7% on LibriSpeechMix-1spk but "degrades heavily on real meetings (30.3% on NSF-1)"; using in-domain close-talk sources gives 18.3% AMI-SDM and 22.1% NSF-1; "a diverse mixture of source domains consistently outperforms any single-domain source" [P]
- Same paper, overlap amount is task dependent: boosting overlap gave the best synthetic-only DiCoW result (22.1% NSF-1, 2.7 abs better than baseline) but worsened Sortformer diarization macro DER 26.1 to 27.6 [P]
- Shi et al. 2024 (Speech-LLM): pre-train on 830 h LibriMix then fine-tune on AMI-SDM reaches cpWER 21.0% (20.4% with beam 4) versus 21.2% for the 900K-hour AED and 21.4% for Whisper-large fine-tuned on AMI. But it is worse than those on 3- and 4-speaker groups (31.1 / 44.1 vs 25.7 / 35.5), which the authors attribute to LibriMix containing only two speakers — [Shi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17431) [P]
- SpeakerLM: "the ASR performance on simulated data is consistently better than that on real data, which is attributed to the simplicity of the simulated data"; stages 3-4 on real data "are crucial for mitigating domain mismatch" — [SpeakerLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06372) [P]
- DiCoW: the multi-domain model (AMI:NOTSOFAR:Libri2Mix = 4:4:1) "performs worse on Libri2Mix, suggesting the domain mismatch between real-world and synthetic mixtures"; authors state results "should be reported on real and not synthetic datasets" — [DiCoW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00114) [P]
- Li et al. 2023: the Whisper models fine-tuned on only 66 h of real AMI came "very close" to the 900K-hour system overall (Whisper medium 23.6%) but lagged on 3- and 4-speaker groups; authors attribute this to missing large-scale simulated SOT pre-training — [Li et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18747) [P]
- Chan & Lian's abstract claims synthetic data "can effectively substitute" real multi-speaker recordings, but evaluation is on LibriSpeechMix, itself synthetic and in-domain — [ScienceDirect abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0885230826000471) [S]

### Inferences
- The Chan & Lian result (2.48% / 2.09%) and the Mind the Gap row "LibriSpeech-source synthetic: 1.7% on LSMix but 30.3% on NOTSOFAR" are two views of the same phenomenon: synthetic-only training looks excellent when the test set is built the same way. The team's own held-out set should include real overlapped Taigi/Mandarin speech, not only self-made mixtures.
- Kanda 2021's decomposition suggests the cheapest large win is in-domain source speech with random overlap (44.2 to 29.0), which the team already has; real overlap patterns add a further 6 points and need either real recordings or a pattern model as in Yang et al.
- Evidence that "more synthetic data hurt" is about share and style, not absolute hours: no reviewed paper shows a monotonic WER increase from adding hours of a well-matched simulation.

### Gaps
- No Whisper-based or Speech-LLM-based study reports synthetic-only vs synthetic+real on Mandarin real meetings (AliMeeting/AISHELL-4) with SOT output; SpeakerLM and DM-ASR are Mandarin but use real data at scale.
- Nguyen & Waibel 2024 and Cornell et al. 2024, both directly on Whisper + synthetic conversations, were not read.

## Curriculum on overlap, single- vs multi-speaker sample ratio, and how much overlap data is enough

### Takeaway
I found no paper that ablates an easy-to-hard overlap-ratio curriculum for multi-talker ASR; "curricula" in this literature are module-unfreezing or data-type stages. The single:multi sampling ratio has direct evidence (50:50 to 67:33 costs or gains only tenths of a point on either side), and data-amount evidence spans from 66 h real being enough to adapt Whisper, to SpeakerLM needing thousands of hours before an end-to-end speaker-attributed model beat cascades.

### Cited Findings
Single:multi ratio
- t-SOT, LibriSpeechMix test WER (1spk / 2spk), all at 160 ms latency: TT-18 single-talker model 4.5 / 63.7; t-SOT TT-18 with 67% 1spk + 33% 2spk 4.7 / 7.4; with 50% + 50% 4.9 / 6.9. TT-36 single-talker 4.4 / 63.3; 67:33 gives 4.4 / 6.3; 50:50 gives 4.3 / 6.2 — [t-SOT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00842) [P]
- Authors' reading: more single-speaker samples give "marginal WER improvement for the single-speaker evaluation set ... with the cost of WER degradation for the two-speaker-mixed evaluation set"; the smaller TT-18 shows "a slight degradation in single-talker WER", the larger TT-36 none [P]
- SOT 2020: training only on 2-speaker mixtures gives 16.7% on single-speaker test vs 5.7% when 1-speaker samples are included [R] — [SOT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12687)
- Kanda 2021 samples the number of speakers uniformly from 1 to 5, i.e. about 20% single-speaker — [Kanda 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16776) [P]
- MT-LLM includes "original single-talker corpora" in its 6.3K h; exact share Not Reported — [MT-LLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08596) [P]
- Chan & Lian's CSV format carries both single-speaker rows (read directly) and two-speaker rows (two paths joined by `_`); the share is Not Retrieved (LFS file) — [repo README](https://github.com/Greg0124/SOT-Whisper-MultiSpeaker-ASR) [P]
- Xiaomi-CocktailASR-1 says the single:multi sampling ratio was tuned to avoid over-suppression in single-speaker input; value Not Reported — [Xiaomi-CocktailASR-1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11274) [P]
- DiCoW weights AMI : NOTSOFAR-1 : Libri2Mix at 4:4:1 (real : synthetic about 8:1) — [DiCoW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00114) [P]; Yang et al.'s best real:simulated ratio is 0.2:0.8 to 0.5:0.5 — [Yang et al. 2023](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/ICASSP2023_Real_Simulation_tSOT.pdf) [P]

Data amount
- Li et al. 2023: 66 h real AMI-SDM, full fine-tune of Whisper medium for 2 epochs, takes AMI-SDM WER from 44.1% (original model) to 23.6% — [Li et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18747) [P/R]
- Same paper, adding simulated LibriSpeech meeting-style data to the adapter model: AMI only 25.0% WER AMI / 43.7% CER AliMeeting; +120 h 24.7 / 40.1; +360 h 24.3 / 36.9. AliMeeting 4-talker counting accuracy 5.2% to 20.0% (+120 h) to 16.3% (+360 h), so gains are not monotonic on every metric [P]
- SpeakerLM SDR data scale, AliMeeting cpCER / AISHELL-4 cpCER as tabulated by DM-ASR: 694 h 29.60 / 25.28; 2,270 h 27.97 / 23.10; 7,639 h 16.05 / 18.37. SpeakerLM's own text: with limited SDR data it "falls behind most cascaded baselines", CER gains from scale are "marginal" because the added in-house data is near-field — [DM-ASR Table](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22467) [P], [SpeakerLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06372) [P]
- DM-ASR with a Qwen3-0.6B decoder, AliMeeting cpCER / AISHELL-4 cpCER [R]: CN 630 h 23.46 / 21.58; CN 1,300 h 21.60 / 19.69; CN+EN 2,900 h 21.73 / 18.96. Authors: "increasing the training data scale leads to substantial and consistent improvements ... in many cases exceeding the gains obtained by scaling model size alone" — [DM-ASR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22467) [P]
- Mind the Gap generated 500 h of synthetic conversation from only about 7.5 h of NOTSOFAR close-talk source and still obtained 22.1% tcpWER on NOTSOFAR-1 (real-only training: 17.7-19.8%) — [Mind the Gap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15442) [P]
- Chan & Lian: more than 8,000 h synthetic from 960 h LibriSpeech, trained for `number_of_epochs: 1`, LR 1e-5, Noam schedule with 500 warm-up steps, batch 64 (medium) / 32 (large), fp16, AdamW weight decay 0.01, grad-norm clip 2.0, test beam 8 — [medium.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Greg0124/SOT-Whisper-MultiSpeaker-ASR/main/hparams/medium.yaml) [P], hours [S]
- Scale of other systems for reference: MT-LLM about 6.3K h; Shi et al. 830 h + AMI 77 h; SOP paper 270 h + 186 h; Whisper-Sidecar LibriSpeech-960-derived; SpeakerLM 5,000 h simulated + 7.7K h real; Xiaomi about 400K h — sources as in the per-paper table.

Curriculum
- No overlap-ratio or SIR curriculum ablation was found in any primary source read. A targeted search returned nothing on this either — [search result summary, no specific source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04152) [S]
- Staged schedules that are reported: SpeakerLM (simulated data for projector alignment, then real data; "Training on simulated data allows the projectors to establish an initial coarse alignment under a simplified distribution") — [SpeakerLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06372) [P]; Shi et al. (projector, then encoder, then LoRA) — [Shi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17431) [P]; DiCoW (CTC preheat, FDDT preheat, full FT) — [DiCoW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00114) [P]; Mind the Gap (synthetic then real, 8.7 vs 8.8 macro for joint training) — [Mind the Gap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15442) [P]
- Li et al. deliberately made simulated data "have more utterance overlaps than the AMI-SDM segments" "to promote the learning of multi-talker recognition" — [Li et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18747) [P]; SE-DiCoW uses overlap ratios 0.8-1.0 — [SE-DiCoW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19194) [P]

### Inferences
- A 50:50 to 67:33 single:multi sampling range is the only ratio with controlled evidence, and it comes from a from-scratch English transducer; for a pretrained model that must also retain three languages, a higher single-speaker share is a defensible starting point but is not directly supported by an ablation.
- Because on-the-fly mixing makes "hours of overlap data" a function of training steps, the more meaningful quantity is hours and diversity of source speech. Evidence that hundreds of hours of source speech suffice for 2-speaker SOT: 270 h Libri2Mix, 830 h ESPnet LibriMix and 960 h LibriSpeech all produced working systems.
- The Chan & Lian "8,000 hours" figure is roughly 8x re-mixing of 960 h source audio; I could not confirm how much of the gain came from hours versus the pairing/length strategies.

### Gaps
- No controlled hours-vs-WER curve for synthetic SOT data on a pretrained model was read (the Chan & Lian curve exists but was not accessible).
- No overlap-ratio curriculum study exists in the sources reviewed; this is an open question rather than a settled one.

## Catastrophic forgetting of single-speaker and multilingual ability, and mitigations

### Takeaway
Forgetting is real and measured: full fine-tuning Whisper on English-only multi-talker data destroyed most of its Mandarin multi-talker gain potential (AliMeeting CER 50.7% vs 28.3% in-language upper bound), and an SOT fine-tune of a streaming model tripled its single-speaker WER. Mitigations with evidence are adapters/frozen backbone, encoder-side conditioning instead of output-format change, and keeping single-speaker data in the mix.

### Cited Findings
Multilingual forgetting
- Li et al. 2023, Whisper medium, AMI-SDM WER / AliMeeting CER: original model 44.0 / 59.1; fine-tuned on AliMeeting (31.3 h) 39.4 / 28.3; **full FT on AMI only 23.6 / 50.7**; adapter on AMI only 25.0 / 43.7; adapter + 120 h simulated English 24.7 / 40.1; adapter + 360 h simulated English 24.3 / 36.9 — [Li et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18747) [P]
- Authors: "naively fine-tuning the model with the English-only multi-talker data impairs the multi-lingual capability"; in preliminary experiments the fully fine-tuned model "performed poorly for other languages" [P]
- Note the trade: the adapter cost 1.4 WER points on the in-language AMI test (23.6 to 25.0) and still left Mandarin 8.6 CER points short of in-language fine-tuning (36.9 vs 28.3) [P]
- Whisper-Sidecar (Whisper frozen, trained on English mixtures only), two-speaker Aishell1Mix CER: small 55.87, medium 36.28, large 28.94 zero-shot; 28.95 / 19.83 / 17.81 after what the paper calls "just one batch tuning" [R, column meaning inferred from text] — [Whisper-Sidecar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09817)
- BUT MLC-SLM system: "even though DiCoW was fine-tuned exclusively on English data, it retains Whisper's multilingual capabilities", attributed to encoder-side diarization conditioning — [BUT MLC-SLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13414) [P]

Single-speaker degradation
- NVIDIA 2026, same 0.6B base model, Open ASR leaderboard average WER: base 7.16; speaker-conditioned SSA 7.44; **word-level SOT fine-tune 16.95**. Per set for WL-SOT vs base: LibriSpeech clean 9.31 vs 2.31, other 18.72 vs 4.75, Earnings 37.25 vs 12.48, AMI 14.64 vs 11.58. Authors: "the WL-SOT model exhibits significant degradation in single-speaker accuracy" — [NVIDIA streaming study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10265) [P]
- DiCoW vs original Whisper large-v3-turbo (greedy, no CTC): LibriSpeech clean 2.5 to 2.1, other 4.5 to 4.3, TED-LIUM 4.3 to 5.3, VoxPopuli 10.9 to 11.2; with CTC joint decoding TED-LIUM rises to 8.5. Authors: the method "slightly degrades its domain generalization capabilities"; improvement on LibriSpeech is "likely due to the inclusion of the LibriSpeech training data" — [DiCoW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00114) [P]
- MT-LLM (frozen encoders and LLM, LoRA r=32, single-talker data kept in training): LibriSpeech test-clean 2.3%; authors contrast with SOT-Conformer at 3.6%, whose "specialization in multi-talker ASR impairs its single-talker performance" — [MT-LLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08596) [P]
- From-scratch evidence that SOT need not hurt: SOT 2020 "did not show any degradation for the single-speaker case" (5.4 vs 5.7); t-SOT TT-36 4.3-4.4 vs single-talker 4.4 — [SOT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12687), [t-SOT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00842) [P]
- Yang et al.: changing only the simulation style moved single-speaker-region WER by 1-2 points (24.5 to 26.6), and blending simulation styles restored it (25.0) — [Yang et al. 2023](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/ICASSP2023_Real_Simulation_tSOT.pdf) [P]
- Chan & Lian report 2.09% single-speaker after full fine-tuning Whisper with single-speaker rows in the training CSV; this is in-domain LibriSpeech only — [SSRN abstract](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5943521) [S]

Mitigations with some evidence
- Bottleneck adapters with frozen backbone — [Li et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18747) [P]
- Frozen backbone + external separator module — [Whisper-Sidecar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09817) [P]
- Frozen LLM/encoders + LoRA + single-talker replay — [MT-LLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08596) [P]
- Encoder-side speaker conditioning with identity-preserving initialisation instead of changing the output format — [DiCoW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00114), [NVIDIA streaming study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10265) [P]
- Tuning the single:multi sampling ratio — [Xiaomi-CocktailASR-1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11274) [P], [t-SOT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00842) [P]
- General Whisper continual-learning results outside multi-talker work (LoRA closer to baseline than full FT on untouched languages; smaller Whisper models forget more; EWC and orthogonal-gradient methods) were surfaced by search only — ["Towards Rehearsal-Free Multilingual ASR"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10680), ["Learn and Don't Forget"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06800) [S]

### Inferences
- The two measured failures (Li et al. full FT; NVIDIA WL-SOT) both evaluated out of the fine-tuning distribution, while the "no degradation" claims (SOT 2020, t-SOT, Chan & Lian, DiCoW on LibriSpeech) are in-distribution. The team should therefore test single-speaker regression on data not used as mixture source, per language.
- Li et al.'s result predicts a specific risk for this project: if overlapped training data covers only some language pairs (say Mandarin+Mandarin), the multi-talker skill may transfer poorly to Taigi and Taigi single-speaker accuracy may drop; the adapter result plus "more overlapped data helps cross-lingual transfer" (36.9 vs 43.7) suggests PEFT and broad language coverage in the mixtures both matter.
- The NVIDIA WL-SOT collapse may partly reflect limited SOT training data (the authors list "scaling the WL-SOT training data" as future work), so it is an upper-range warning rather than an expected outcome.

### Gaps
- No paper reports single-speaker WER in several languages before and after multi-talker fine-tuning of a multilingual model. Li et al. test only multi-talker Mandarin.
- No measurement of forgetting for a Speech-LLM (encoder+projector+LLM) under encoder unfreezing vs LLM-only LoRA was found.
- No evidence for low-resource languages such as Taiwanese Hokkien.

## LoRA / PEFT versus full fine-tuning for multi-talker adaptation

### Takeaway
There is no clean LoRA-vs-full-FT comparison for SOT multi-talker ASR on a pretrained model. The nearest evidence (target-speaker ASR on Libri2Mix, about 58 h-scale data) shows LoRA beating full fine-tuning, and Speech-LLM ablations show LLM LoRA helps by 0.5-1.4 WER while the encoder must be adapted to overlap or results collapse (46.7% WER with a frozen un-adapted encoder).

### Cited Findings
- Ma et al., Whisper target-speaker ASR on Libri2Mix, WER test-clean / test-both and trainable params: small deep-prompt 24.62 / 45.46 (0.69M); small LoRA 19.06 / 36.82 (0.89M); **small full FT 23.18 / 41.39 (241M)**; medium deep-prompt 13.89 / 29.81 (1.31M); medium LoRA 11.98 / 26.38 (1.85M); **medium full FT 18.79 / 34.04 (763M)**; large deep-prompt 14.82 / 30.19; large LoRA 13.07 / 26.51 (2.87M) — [prompt tuning TS-ASR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08079) [P]
- SQ-Whisper reports a mixed picture on the same task: for the TSE-Whisper baseline LoRA 25.6 beat full FT 28.3, and the authors say LoRA models outperform fully fine-tuned ones "across all adaptation methods" with about 3% trainable parameters, attributing it to over-fitting; yet the listed SQ-Whisper numbers are full FT 20.1 vs LoRA 23.2. The snippet is internally inconsistent and needs checking — [SQ-Whisper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05589) [S]
- Li et al. 2023 (SOT on Whisper medium, AMI): full FT 23.6% vs frozen+adapters 25.0% in-language; adapters far better cross-lingually (43.7 vs 50.7 CER) — [Li et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18747) [P]
- Shi et al. 2024, LibriMix test WER with Vicuna-7B, LoRA off / on: WavLM Base+ 17.3 / 15.9; WavLM Large 11.3 / 10.2; WavLM Large pre-fine-tuned on LibriMix 9.5 / 9.0 — [Shi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17431) [P]
- Same paper, encoder freezing: un-adapted WavLM Large frozen **46.7%** vs jointly trained 10.2%; LibriMix-adapted encoder frozen 9.0% vs jointly trained 10.1%. Multi-stage (projector, encoder, LoRA) beat single-stage by 0.2-0.3 (10.1 vs 10.4; 9.0 vs 9.2) [P]
- MT-LLM with all backbones frozen and only adapters + projector + LoRA r=32 (1% of parameters) reaches 5.2% on LibriSpeechMix-2spk vs 4.9% for a fully trained SOT-Conformer, and 10.2% vs 6.2% on 3-speaker; authors call the gap "understandable" for an exploratory system. Its encoder side includes WavLM, without which 3-speaker WER is 16.1% — [MT-LLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08596) [P]
- Whisper-Sidecar with 1.2-3.5% trainable parameters and a frozen Whisper: LibriSpeechMix-2spk 3.81% (large), Libri2Mix 4.98% [R] — [Whisper-Sidecar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09817)
- Full fine-tuning at scale works when data is large and in-domain: Chan & Lian (full FT, 8,000+ h synthetic, 2.48% LibriSpeechMix-2spk) [S]; DiCoW final stage is full FT at a very low LR (2e-7 base) [P] — [Chan & Lian abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0885230826000471), [DiCoW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00114)
- LoRA configurations actually used in Qwen-family multi-speaker work: DM-ASR r=16, alpha=32 on frozen Whisper-turbo or Qwen3-ASR-1.7B encoder and Qwen3 decoder, projector trained, peak LR 1e-4; Speaker-Reasoner r=8, scaling 32 on all linear layers of encoder + projector + LLM, LR 1e-5; SpeakerLM LoRA on Qwen2.5-7B with encoder fully fine-tuned in stage 3 — [DM-ASR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22467), [Speaker-Reasoner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03074), [SpeakerLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06372) [P]
- Learning rates used for full FT of Whisper on multi-talker data are small: 1e-6 (Li et al.), 1e-5 (Chan & Lian), 2e-7 (DiCoW backbone) — sources above [P]

### Inferences
- For Qwen3-ASR-0.6B the Shi et al. frozen-encoder result is the most decision-relevant single number: an audio encoder never trained on overlap, left frozen, gave 46.7% WER even with a 7B LLM being adapted. LoRA (or full FT) should therefore reach the audio encoder and projector, not only the LLM. Speaker-Reasoner applying LoRA to all encoder linear layers is a precedent in the Qwen family.
- The TS-ASR finding that LoRA beats full FT comes from small training sets (Libri2Mix train-100) where over-fitting dominates; with thousands of hours of on-the-fly mixtures (Chan & Lian) full FT is competitive. Which regime applies depends on how much Taigi source speech exists.
- LoRA's stronger argument in this project is retention (Li et al. adapter result) rather than raw overlapped accuracy, where full FT was 1.4 points better in-language.

### Gaps
- No paper compares LoRA rank, or LoRA placement (encoder vs projector vs LLM), for multi-talker SOT.
- No LoRA vs full-FT comparison exists on real meeting data with a Speech-LLM.
- The SQ-Whisper numbers need to be verified in the paper itself.

## Multilingual, cross-lingual and code-switching overlap experiments; Taiwanese Hokkien / Mandarin work

### Takeaway
Only one reviewed system trains on mixtures where the overlapped speakers use different languages (MT-LLM, English+German, about 10% of 6.3K h), and it works but at 4x the monolingual error rate. No multi-talker or overlapped-speech work on Taiwanese Hokkien was found, and no study varies language pairs in overlapped mixtures.

### Cited Findings
- MT-LLM builds cross-lingual mixtures by mixing CoVoST 2 German (180 h) with LibriSpeech utterances; about 10% of the 6.3K h training audio contains German. WER: LibriSpeech 1-spk 2.3; LibriSpeechMix 2-spk 5.2, 3-spk 10.2; German single-talker 11.7 (XLSR-Large-De reference 12.1); **En-De-Mix 2-spk 21.0, 3-spk 22.8** — [MT-LLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08596) [P]
- Authors: German audio is "relatively limited and noisier than LibriSpeech", yet the model shows "ability to simultaneously distinguish and transcribe multiple languages from multi-talker speech"; it also supports a "target-lingual" instruction (transcribe only the English or only the German talker) — [MT-LLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08596), [repo demo table](https://github.com/cuhealthybrains/MT-LLM) [P]
- Cross-lingual transfer of the multi-talker skill (English training, Mandarin test): Li et al. full FT 50.7% CER, adapters 43.7%, adapters + 360 h simulated English 36.9%, in-language 28.3% — [Li et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18747) [P]
- Whisper-Sidecar English-trained, zero-shot Mandarin two-speaker Aishell1Mix: 28.94% CER with Whisper-large, 17.81% after minimal tuning [R] — [Whisper-Sidecar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09817)
- DiCoW trained on English only retained multilingual ability and was then fine-tuned on MLC-SLM data — [BUT MLC-SLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13414) [P]
- MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize claims 50+ languages and first place in the 2nd MLC-SLM Challenge (14 languages) at Interspeech 2026; its Movies test set is "primarily Chinese and English, but also covers ... Korean, Japanese, and Cantonese" — [MOSS repo](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize), [MOSS report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01554) [P]
- DM-ASR trains one model on Mandarin + English meeting data (CN+EN 2,900 h / 7,500 h); for the 0.6B decoder, adding English to 1,300 h Mandarin changed AliMeeting cpCER 21.60 to 21.73 and AISHELL-4 19.69 to 18.96 [R] — [DM-ASR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22467)
- An older end-to-end system (MERL, 2019) trained on "multilingual overlapped speech mixture with code-switching" — [MERL TR2019-101](https://www.merl.com/publications/docs/TR2019-101.pdf) [S]
- Mandarin simulated-mixture resource: Aishell1Mix (LibriMix-style from AISHELL-1) — [Aishell1Mix](https://github.com/huangzj421/Aishell1Mix) [P, existence only]
- Taiwanese Hokkien: searches returned only single-speaker resources (TaigiSpeech intent dataset with 21 speakers / 3k utterances; Breeze Taigi trained on about 10,000 h synthetic Taigi speech; TAT; YouTube drama corpus). No overlapped or multi-speaker Taigi dataset or paper surfaced — [TaigiSpeech](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21478), [Breeze Taigi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19259), [CLiFT-ASR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06860) [S]

### Inferences
- The En-De-Mix result (21.0%) versus monolingual 2-speaker (5.2%) should not be read as "cross-lingual overlap is 4x harder": the German data was a 10% minority, noisier, and from a different corpus. It does establish that an SOT-style Speech-LLM can emit two languages in one serialized output without architectural change.
- Given Li et al., a Taigi+Mandarin capability is unlikely to emerge from Mandarin+Mandarin and English+English mixtures alone; the mixtures should explicitly include Taigi in both overlapped positions. This follows from the cross-lingual numbers but has not been tested for closely related language pairs sharing a script.
- Taigi+Mandarin overlap raises an output-format question no reviewed paper faces (both languages written in Han characters, so per-speaker language is not recoverable from script as it is for En-De).

### Gaps
- No experiment anywhere on overlapped mixtures of two Sinitic languages, on Taiwanese Hokkien multi-talker ASR, or on code-switching inside one speaker's turn combined with overlap.
- No study varies the proportion of same-language vs cross-language mixtures.
- MLC-SLM challenge system papers (multilingual conversational Speech-LLMs) were not reviewed in detail.
