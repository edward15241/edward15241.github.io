## KQ1. Hao Shi et al.: SLT 2024 (EncSep/GEncSep), ASRU 2025 (SOP), follow-ups, and the code

### Takeaway
Architecture: WavLM-Large → {3 conv downsamplers + projector → LLaMA prefix (SOT)} plus a ~14M-param 2-layer LSTM **Separator** with one CTC head per talker, trained with **fixed onset order (no PIT)**; SOP feeds greedy CTC text embeddings *in front of* the mixed speech embeddings. The ablations say: (1) adding the separator+CTC loss alone ("SOT-CTC", stage 2) **hurts** the LLM path in 2-talker conditions (e.g. 11.3→13.4 noisy Libri2Mix eval, 1B); (2) SOP gives small 2-talker gains (11.3→10.5) and larger 3-talker gains (39.1→28.5); (3) encoder-only serialized CTC on a **frozen** SOT-adapted WavLM is about equal to LLM-SOT for 2 talkers (10.1 vs 9.1 noisy) and far better for 3 talkers (20.5 vs 32.3) — a clean demonstration that the 3-talker failure is on the decoder/interface side, not in the encoder.

### Cited Findings

**SLT 2024 — EncSep / GEncSep (arXiv 2409.00815)**
- Setup [paper]: ESPnet `librimix/sot_asr1` recipe, Conformer AED back-end, **WavLM-Large used as a frozen feature extractor ("During training, all parameters of the WavLM-Large were frozen")**, character vocabulary of 32 incl. `<sc>`. Separator: `Ĥ = LayerNorm(LSTM(H))`, `H_sep^s = ReLU(Linear_s(Ĥ))`; 2 LSTM layers, separator input/output dims 256/512, per-speaker linear 512→256 (BiLSTM variant: 1024→256). 60 epochs, 10-checkpoint average. — [arXiv 2409.00815](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.00815)
- CTC target assignment [paper]: "Linear layers correspond to a speaker according to the start time of speaking in a serialized manner" — i.e. head *s* ↔ *s*-th speaker in SOT (start-time) order; **no PIT** (uPIT is described only as related work). Loss: `L_EncSep = γ·ΣCTC_s + (1−γ)·L_SOT` (γ value Not Reported). — [arXiv 2409.00815](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.00815)
- Inference [paper]: for **EncSep** "The separator is only used to introduce CTC information during training… EncSep does not increase any computational cost during decoding". For **GEncSep** the separated streams are concatenated over time, `H_con = Concat(H_sep^1..H_sep^S)`, and the decoder's cross-attention attends to `H_con` (separator needed at inference; initialised from EncSep). Training note: "pretraining is unnecessary for the two-mixed condition, but a few epochs of original SOT training are needed for the three-mixed condition." — [arXiv 2409.00815](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.00815)

**ASRU 2025 — Serialized Output Prompting (arXiv 2509.04488)**
- Architecture [paper]: speech encoder WavLM-Large ("as WavLM includes MT data in its pre-training"); three CNN downsampling layers, each 2× (`H_d = Conv3(Conv2(Conv1(H_e)))`); **Separator taps the output of the 2nd CNN layer** (`H_sep = Separator(H^(2))`) — "the final layer applies excessive downsampling", while the raw encoder output "is relatively long, which increases computation time and CTC merging time"; per-talker `ReLU(Linear_s(LayerNorm(H_sep)))` → CTC; greedy decode each branch, concatenate → `C_sop`; embed with the LLM embedding layer → `E_sop`; decoder input **`H_sop = [E_sop ; H_p ; E_t]`** (SOP text embeddings, projected mixed speech, text). LLMs: LLaMA-3.2-1B, 3.2-3B, 3.1-8B. Data: Libri2Mix 270 h, Libri3Mix 186 h, with ESPnet offset files for different start times. — [arXiv 2509.04488](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04488)
- Three stages [paper]: **Stage 1** SOT fine-tuning — trains the speech encoder and the LLM (LoRA), CE loss, LoRA then merged. **Stage 2** — "jointly optimizes: the speech encoder, CNN-based downsampling layers, the newly inserted Separator, and serialized CTC layers" with the hybrid loss "applied not only to the CTC branch, but also to the LLM output". **Stage 3** — a new set of LoRA modules, "Only the LoRA parameters are trainable", CE loss, input `[E_sop; H_p; E_t]`. Motivation: "We attempted to train all parameters directly in a single stage, but the system performance was suboptimal… CTC training affects the performance of the LLM-based decoder when they are trained simultaneously… the Separator, CTC layers, and speech encoder can be directly trained effectively in the two-talker condition; however, in the three-talker condition, the Separator and CTC layers need to be trained using a speech encoder that has been finetuned with SOT." Hybrid-loss weight, LoRA rank, lr: Not Reported in the paper text I read (code defaults below). — [arXiv 2509.04488](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04488)

- Authors' diagnosis [paper]: SOT-CTC degradation "may be due to the presence of <blank> tokens in CTC, which lead to sparse speech embeddings… the sparsity introduced by CTC made it more difficult for the CNNs to extract meaningful information"; the damage "can be mitigated in the third training stage". On 3-mix, "LLM-based approaches underperformed on Libri3Mix relative to traditional AED E2E ASR systems" (e.g. GEncSep 25.9 noisy / 13.1 clean eval vs SOT-LLaMA-3B 31.7 / 22.0) because "LLM-based SOT-ASR systems rely solely on self-attention. **Follow-up A — "Beyond Acoustic Prefixes: Persistent Access to Serialized Acoustic Memory…" (arXiv 2603.27205)**
- Pipeline [paper]: (a) *SOT backbone*: "The WavLM encoder, temporal-reduction module, and modality projector were trainable, whereas the dense LLM parameters remained frozen. LoRA [r=16, α=32, dropout 0.1] was applied to the query, key, value, and output projections of self-attention in every LLM layer… The input embeddings and LM output head were also updated." (b) *Serialized-CTC initialisation*: "The existing SOT pathway, including WavLM and the LLM decoder, was frozen, and only the separator and CTC heads were updated… exclusively using L_SerCTC; no SOT loss". (c) Stage 1: only decoder-side cross-attention adapters (+ gates) trained; (d) Stage 2: new LoRA (r_max=8) on cross-attention and optionally self-attention. AdamW, lr 3e-5, warmup 400, 50 epochs, 8 GPUs. — [arXiv 2603.27205](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27205)
- Separator spec [paper]: 2-layer LSTM, hidden 796, dropout 0.2, LayerNorm, K talker branches `796→796→1024` with ReLU + LayerNorm; each CTC head is a linear `1024 → 128,265` classes (LLM tokenizer vocabulary + blank). Parameter breakdown (Table IX-b): WavLM 315.5M; adapter+projector 21.0M (1B); **Separator 13.9M / 15.3M** (2-/3-talker); **CTC heads 262.9M / 394.4M**; cross-attention + gates 50.5M (1B), 117.9M (3B), 168.5M (8B). "The CTC heads are used only during training for the LLM-decoding systems and are removed for deployment." — [arXiv 2603.27205](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27205)

### Inferences
- [inference] The "serialized-CTC initialisation" phase is, in effect, the probing experiment the team wants: freeze the encoder, train a 14M LSTM + linear CTC per talker, decode by CTC only. On an SOT-adapted WavLM-Large this read-out reaches 10.1 (noisy) / 4.6 (clean) on Libri2Mix — proof that both talkers' lexical content is recoverable from the top-layer, 50 Hz states of that encoder.
- [inference] For **two talkers** these papers show no decoder-side deficit once the encoder has been SOT-trained (LLM-SOT 9.1/3.7 ≤ CTC read-out 10.1/4.6). For **three talkers** the deficit is clearly at the prefix/LLM interface (32.3 vs 20.5), and static prompts (SOP) recover little of it in the later, better-controlled paper (31.3), while persistent cross-attention recovers most (17.4).
- [inference] None of these recipes keeps the speech encoder at its pretrained weights: the SOT stage always trains WavLM (and input embeddings + LM head) with LoRA on the LLM. They therefore do not tell us what a *frozen pretrained* encoder contains (see KQ4 for that).
- [inference] SOP's 2-talker gain (≈0.6–0.8 abs. on noisy eval) came bundled with a stage-2 that first *degraded* the SOT path by 1–3 points; the later paper's cleaner recipe (separator+CTC trained on a frozen encoder, so the SOT path is untouched) avoids that side-effect.
- [inference] The two layer ablations (WavLM layer 12/18/24 as acoustic source; 6/12/18/24 encoder layers for LLM-SOT) both show monotonic improvement with depth *for an SOT-fine-tuned encoder*, i.e. fine-tuning makes the upper layers do the talker disentanglement. This is the opposite of what is seen in frozen single-talker encoders (Sidecar location ablation, KQ2/KQ4).

- γ / hybrid-loss weight, LoRA rank and learning rate are not stated in the SLT/ASRU paper text I read; values above are code defaults.
- No experiment in this line with a frozen-from-pretraining encoder, or with a non-WavLM (Whisper/AuT-style) encoder.

## KQ2. Other multi-speaker encoder-side designs

### Takeaway
(1) **In-encoder separators with multi-branch output + PIT** (Sidecar on wav2vec2/Whisper; SURT) keep a single-talker model frozen and run the rest of the model once per talker; (2) **single-stream SOT with an auxiliary CTC** (SOT-CTC, SACTC) shapes the encoder and costs nothing at inference; (3) **mixture-aware pre-training** (WavLM utterance mixing, Cocktail HuBERT) decides whether a second talker was ever represented. Fixed-order assignment (HEAT, serialized CTC) is consistently equal or better than PIT.

### Cited Findings
- **Sidecar (Meng et al., ICASSP 2023, arXiv 2302.09908)** [paper]: frozen wav2vec 2.0-base CTC ASR; a Conv-TasNet-style TCN (128 bottleneck channels, 768 in/out) plus two 3-kernel conv layers predicts speaker-dependent **masks** on the mixed embedding; separated embeddings are concatenated on the batch dimension and pass through the remaining frozen layers; PIT with CTC loss only. Trainable: **8.7M (8.4% of all parameters)**. Libri2Mix-clean WER dev/test: baseline (duplicate-layer embedding prediction) 11.60 / 12.27; **W2V-Sidecar 9.76 / 10.36**; full fine-tune W2V-Sidecar-ft 7.68 / 8.12. LibriSpeechMix-2spk: 7.76 / 7.56 (frozen), 6.01 / 5.69 (ft). Adding a reconstruction loss barely matters (10.16 with SI-SNR). — [arXiv 2302.09908](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.09908)
- **Sidecar location ablation** [paper, Table 3, frozen wav2vec2, Libri2Mix test WER by insertion point (0 = before block 1 … 12 = after the last block)]: **13.01, 11.87, 10.36, 12.65, 16.88, 30.32 (loc 6), 57.11 (loc 9), 62.72 (loc 12)**. Authors: "deeper locations show a dramatic decrease… the separation is better performed on acoustic-related representations… if the ASR encoder goes deeper, the speaker information will be eliminated." — [arXiv 2302.09908](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.09908)
- **Whisper + Sidecar + TTI (Meng et al., Interspeech 2024, arXiv 2407.09817)** [paper]: Whisper-small/medium/large-v3 **frozen**; Sidecar (K=8 TCN blocks, R=3 repeats) "plug[ged] between the second and third encoder blocks", talker masks multiplied onto the mixed embedding, "The subsequent encoder blocks and decoder process these branches"; Target Talker Identifier (two linear layers on the 3-s enrollment prefix); learnable decoder soft prompt (best length 4); loss `L_ASR(PIT) + 0.01·L_TTI`; no CTC ("the original Whisper was not trained using CTC loss"). - **Sidecar + diarization (arXiv 2305.16263)**: adds a diarization branch with "only 768 parameters" [search-extract]. — [arXiv 2305.16263](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.16263)
- **SURT / HEAT (Lu, Kanda, Li, Gong, arXiv 2011.13148)** [paper]: RNN-T with an unmixing module (speaker-differentiator or mask-based) and two output channels. HEAT: `L = L_rnnt(Y1,H1) + L_rnnt(Y2,H2)` with `Y1` always the utterance spoken first. WER dev-clean (τ=0 / τ=0.5 eval; trained with τ=0.5): SD-encoder PIT 12.0 / 11.3 vs HEAT 11.8 / 10.9; mask w/o MixEnc PIT 14.1 / 13.8 vs HEAT 13.4 / 12.3; mask w/ MixEnc HEAT 10.1 / 9.5. SURT 81M, 150 ms latency: 10.8 test-clean vs offline PIT-S2S (160.7M) 11.1. - **Graph-PIT (von Neumann et al., Interspeech 2021, arXiv 2107.14446)** [search-extract]: assigns utterances to output channels by graph colouring so that only the maximum number of *concurrently* active speakers must fit the number of outputs; generalises uPIT for meeting-length separation. — [arXiv 2107.14446](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.14446), [code](https://github.com/fgnt/graph_pit)
- **Speaker-Aware CTC (Kang, Meng, et al., arXiv 2409.12388)** [paper]: finding — "CTC guides the encoder to represent different speakers in distinct temporal regions of acoustic embeddings"; SACTC (Bayes-risk CTC with a speaker-aware risk) used as an auxiliary loss for SOT. LibriSpeechMix-2mix test WER (overall; low / mid / high overlap): SOT 9.2 (9.0 / 8.0 / 12.8); CTC only 11.1 (7.5 / 12.4 / 18.2); **SOT+CTC 8.8** (7.1 / 9.0 / 13.1); SACTC only 12.3; **SOT+SACTC 8.0** (6.0 / 8.4 / 12.8). Abstract: 10% relative WER reduction overall, 15% on low-overlap speech vs SOT-CTC. — [arXiv 2409.12388](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12388), [code](https://github.com/kjw11/Speaker-Aware-CTC)
- **WavLM utterance mixing (arXiv 2110.13900)** [search-extract]: pre-training adds simulated overlapped utterances with the *main* speaker's pseudo-labels as target; overlap kept < 50%; mixing probability 0 for Base, 10% for Base+/Large; HuBERT "lacks multi-speaker modeling during pre-training". — [arXiv 2110.13900](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.13900v5)
- **Cocktail HuBERT (arXiv 2303.11131)**: masked pseudo source separation pre-training predicting units of all sources; C-HuBERT-Large reaches 6.6 dev / 7.8 test WER on clean Libri2Mix as quoted in comparison tables [paper tables of 2409.00815 and 2407.09817]; abstract claims 69% lower multi-speaker ASR WER than prior SSL models [search-extract]. — [arXiv 2409.00815 Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.00815), [ResearchGate abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374947491_Cocktail_Hubert_Generalized_Self-Supervised_Pre-Training_for_Mixture_and_Single-Source_Speech)

### Inferences
- [inference] Inference cost: Sidecar ≈ lower layers once + (remaining encoder + decoder) × N talkers, and N must be fixed in advance; SOT (+aux CTC) = one pass; SOP = one pass + LSTM separator + longer prompt; cross-attention memory ≈ +55–75% latency in Shi's Table IX.
- [inference] The Whisper-Sidecar zero-shot Mandarin result (CER 28.94 with zero Mandarin multi-talker training) shows that an in-encoder separator learned on English transfers partially across languages because the frozen upper layers and decoder keep Whisper's multilingual ability — relevant for a Mandarin/multilingual Qwen3-ASR target.
- [inference] HEAT's observation that a short non-overlapped onset is a strong cue matches Shi et al.'s use of onset-ordered labels with start-time offsets; data with fully synchronous starts gives fixed-order training no ordering cue.

### Gaps
- "Surround/multi-turn RNN-T", BA-SOT (arXiv 2305.13716), SA-SOT (arXiv 2403.02010), SD-CTC (arXiv 2506.07515) and Graph-PIT were not read in full; no numbers reported here for them.

## KQ3. NVIDIA Multitalker Parakeet Streaming 0.6B / Self-Speaker Adaptation, META-CAT, DiCoW

### Takeaway
These are **diarization-conditioned target-speaker encoders**: a tiny injection module masks an early encoder layer with one speaker's frame-level activity, and one model instance is run **per speaker**. They are state of the art on overlapped benchmarks but need a diarizer (Sortformer) and N passes, and activity masks become ambiguous when speakers overlap completely — they do not fit a diarization-free, single-pass requirement.

### Cited Findings
- **Math** [paper, arXiv 2506.22646, Wang, Park, Medennikov et al., Interspeech 2025]: `X_inj^i = f_inj(X^i, y_spk^k) + X^i`, with `X^i ∈ R^{B×T×D}` the output of layer *i*, `y_spk^k ∈ (0,1)^{B×T×1}` the speech activity of speaker *k*, and `f_inj(X, y) = FeedForward(X ⊙ y)` — "two linear layers with an activation in between". Injection is at the **pre-encode (subsampling) layer** of FastConformer. "The speech activity is treated as a mask and applied to the output of the selected layer, with a residual connection". — [arXiv 2506.22646](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.22646)
- **Inputs / instances** [paper]: needs only per-speaker speech activity (ground truth or diarizer output; personal VAD → target-speaker ASR; diarizer → multi-speaker ASR); no enrollment or speaker embedding. "this approach requires deploying one model instance per speaker" — implemented as a batch whose size equals the number of speakers. Training: target speaker randomly chosen per utterance, labels = that speaker's transcript; "training process remains nearly identical to that of a conventional single-speaker ASR system"; diarizer frozen. Motivation: single-speaker streaming ASR tends "to prioritize a specific speaker… while disregarding other speakers", and fine-tuning it for multi-talker "must undergo substantial weight adjustments to counteract this inherent bias". — [arXiv 2506.22646](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.22646)
- **Size / data** [paper]: 238M = FastConformer-Transducer 114M + Sortformer 123M + **speaker injection module 1.1M**. Training data simulated from LibriSpeech (offline: 1-/2-/3-mix ratio 1:3:6; 200k steps, 8×A100), then Fisher fine-tuning (50k steps) for CH109. Sortformer DER on LibriSpeechMix 2-mix / 3-mix: 2.5 / 3.2 offline, 3.3 / 4.1 at 1120 ms. — [arXiv 2506.22646](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.22646)
- **Results** [paper, cpWER]: Offline LibriSpeechMix 1-/2-/3-mix: **SSA 2.2 / 2.8 / 5.0**; vs SOT 4.6 / 11.2 / 24.0; E2E-SA 3.3 / 4.3 / 6.0; MT-Whisper-L (1.56B) – / 3.4 / 6.8; MT-LLM (8B) 2.3 / 5.2 / 10.2; AFT-MT 2.4 / 3.4 / –; Sidecar-Sep – / 5.7 / –. Streaming, max-2-speaker model (1mix / 2mix) by ASR latency: 80 ms 6.8 / 8.3; 160 ms 5.7 / 7.2; 560 ms 4.0 / 5.6; 1120 ms 3.8 / 5.2; 2720 ms 3.4 / 4.6. Max-3-speaker model (1/2/3mix): 560 ms 4.3 / 5.9 / 11.0; 1120 ms 4.0 / 5.4 / 9.8; 2720 ms 3.7 / 4.9 / 8.1. CH109 at 1120 ms: SSA 26.21 vs cascade 27.38 vs offline LLM-BSD (2.2B) 26.31. — [arXiv 2506.22646](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.22646)
- **Model card** [HF README]: "streaming multitalker ASR model based on the Nemotron-Speech-Streaming model"; 600M params; "only takes the speaker diarization outputs as external information"; speaker kernels injected "into the pre-encode layer of the Fast-Conformer encoder"; "requires deploying **one model instance per speaker**"; each instance "receives the same mixed audio input"; paired with `nvidia/diar_streaming_sortformer_4spk-v2(.1)`. Latency via `att_context_size` in 80 ms frames: [70,0] 0.08 s; [70,1] 0.16 s; [70,6] 0.56 s; [70,13] 1.12 s. Input mono 16 kHz. Training data: real conversations (Granary single-speaker, Fisher, LibriSpeech, AMI, NOTSOFAR, ICSI) + LibriSpeech-simulated mixtures. cpWER with Streaming Sortformer v2 at 1.12 s: **AMI IHM 21.26, AMI SDM 37.44, CH109 15.81, Mixer 6 23.81**. License: NVIDIA Open Model License. — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/multitalker-parakeet-streaming-0.6b-v1)
- **DiCoW and SOT-DiCoW (Kocour et al., arXiv 2510.03723)** [paper]: DiCoW decodes each speaker independently, conditioned on STNO (silence / target / non-target / overlap) diarization masks; SA-DiCoW runs the DiCoW encoder per speaker and **concatenates** the per-speaker embeddings for one SOT decoder (≈918M trainable; stage 1 freezes Whisper and trains new parts for 1,000 steps, stage 2 unfreezes all at lr 2e-6). Aggregation ablation (cpWER Libri2Mix-clean / NOTSOFAR): weighted sum 4.8 / 59.1; average 4.6 / 50.2; masked average 4.6 / 47.4; **concatenation 3.9 / 21.0** — "averaging-based methods attenuate the acoustic information across multiple speakers". LibriMix-clean cpWER 2spk / 3spk: DiCoW 4.8 / 32.1; SA-DiCoW 3.9 / 18.0; + speaker loss 3.4 / 17.2. Authors: "in fully overlapped conditions, the STNO masks for different speakers become ambiguous, giving the model little guidance on which speaker to transcribe." — [arXiv 2510.03723](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03723)
- **DiCoW / META-CAT originals** [search-extract]: DiCoW (arXiv 2501.00114) uses frame-level diarization-dependent transformations (four class-specific affine transforms weighted by STNO probabilities) plus query-key biasing; META-CAT (arXiv 2409.12352) masks ASR encoder activations with Sortformer output and concatenates the speaker-specific versions. — [arXiv 2501.00114](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.00114), [arXiv 2409.12352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12352)

### Inferences
- [inference] "No enrollment" ≠ "no diarization": every system here consumes frame-level speaker activity. In the team's target condition (two people speaking simultaneously), activity masks for the two speakers are nearly identical, which is exactly the failure mode the DiCoW authors describe (32.1 cpWER on 3-speaker LibriMix). NVIDIA's good fully-overlapped LibriSpeechMix numbers depend on Sortformer resolving distinct onsets (LibriSpeechMix is partially overlapped with random delays).
- [inference] The 1.1M injection module vs a 114M ASR model shows that an early-layer, very small conditioning signal is enough to steer a single-talker encoder to either speaker — consistent with the Sidecar finding that both speakers are still present in the first few layers.
- [inference] The SA-DiCoW aggregation result (concatenate ≫ sum/average) is the only direct evidence found on "summed vs concatenated streams" and argues against summing separated streams before the decoder.

### Gaps
- Diarization-free variants: "Scaling Multi-Talker ASR with Speaker-Agnostic Activity Streams" (arXiv 2510.03630), "Soft Posterior Speaker Injection" (arXiv 2609.01287) and "Grounding Spoken LLMs… via Diarization Conditioning" (arXiv 2606.18134) surfaced only as titles and were not read.
- NVIDIA does not report which other layers ("selected encoder layers") were tried, nor an ablation on injection depth.

## KQ4. Does the encoder retain the second speaker? Probing-style evidence and frozen-vs-fine-tuned ablations

### Takeaway
No dedicated probing paper on second-speaker *content* was found, but four published ablations answer the question indirectly and consistently: **the information is present in the early layers of single-talker encoders and is progressively discarded in upper layers unless the encoder is trained on mixtures**. A frozen, un-adapted WavLM-Large with only projector + LLM-LoRA trained gives 46.7% WER on noisy Libri2Mix vs 10.2% when unfrozen; a separator placed at the *top* of a frozen wav2vec2 gives 62.7% vs 10.4% at layer 2; yet frozen Whisper-large-v2 + LoRA-LLM reaches 6.6% on 2-speaker LibriSpeechMix. So "frozen encoder + decoder-only training" can work for a large weakly-supervised encoder on partially-overlapped speech, but is not guaranteed — it must be measured per encoder.

### Cited Findings
- **Frozen vs trainable encoder in an LLM-SOT system (Tencent, arXiv 2408.17431, SLT 2024)** [paper, Table 3; noisy-condition Libri2Mix dev/test WER, WavLM-Large + projector + LLM with LoRA]: encoder **not** LibriMix-fine-tuned & **unfrozen**: 11.4 / 10.2; not fine-tuned & **frozen: 47.8 / 46.7**; AED-SOT-fine-tuned & unfrozen: 11.4 / 10.1; **AED-SOT-fine-tuned & frozen: 10.3 / 9.0** (best). Authors: "When the initialized speech encoder is not fine-tuned on LibriMix using the AED method, freezing the encoder results in poor performance because the encoder has not adapted to the LibriMix dataset"; with the original WavLM, first-stage projector-only training shows "slower and less complete convergence… for an unadapted encoder, merely training the projector to perform alignment is insufficient." — [arXiv 2408.17431](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.17431)
- Same paper, other tables [paper]: LoRA on the LLM vs none (dev/test): WavLM Base+ 19.4 / 17.3 → 17.6 / 15.9; WavLM Large 12.6 / 11.3 → 11.4 / 10.2; fine-tuned WavLM Large 10.8 / 9.5 → 10.3 / 9.0 ("LoRA fine-tuning can adapt the LLM output to the style of SOT"). Multi-stage (projector → encoder → LoRA) vs single-stage: 11.4 / 10.1 vs 11.7 / 10.4 (unfrozen), 10.3 / 9.0 vs 10.5 / 9.2 (frozen). AED with fine-tuned WavLM Large: 10.6 / 9.2; ESPnet with **frozen** WavLM-Large upstream + Conformer: 19.4 / 17.1; ESPnet Whisper-small encoder: 26.0 / 25.0. AMI-SDM: LLM 21.0 cpWER (20.4 with beam 4) vs AED 24.1. — [arXiv 2408.17431](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.17431)
- **Frozen Whisper + frozen WavLM + LoRA-LLM (MT-LLM, arXiv 2409.08596)** [paper]: Whisper-large-v2 encoder (last layer) + WavLM-base (learnable weighted sum of layers), "All parameters of above models are frozen"; adapters = 2 conv layers + bottleneck adapter + linear (80 ms stride, dim 2048); Llama-2-chat-7b with LoRA r=32; 7.55B total, **76.6M (1%) trainable**. Ablation (Table IV; WER LibriSpeech 1-spk / LibriSpeechMix 2-spk / 3-spk): **Whisper encoder only: 4.3 / 6.6 / 16.1**; + WavLM: 2.4 / 5.5 / 10.7; + multi-task training: 2.3 / 5.2 / 10.2. Authors: adding WavLM "leads to notable performance improvements… particularly in three-talker scenarios". Reference: SALMONN-7B (not trained for this) 2.4 / 32.9 / 45.9; SOT-Conformer 3.6 / 4.9 / 6.2. — [arXiv 2409.08596](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08596), [code](https://github.com/cuhealthybrains/mt-llm)
- **Where in a frozen single-talker encoder the two talkers are still separable (Sidecar location ablation)** [paper]: Libri2Mix test WER 10.36 at layer 2, 16.88 at layer 4, 30.32 at layer 6, 57.11 at layer 9, 62.72 at layer 12 of a frozen wav2vec 2.0 CTC model; "if the ASR encoder goes deeper, the speaker information will be eliminated". — [arXiv 2302.09908](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.09908)
- **Frozen Whisper, separator after encoder block 2** [paper]: 4.66 (Libri2Mix-clean, fully overlapped) / 16.79 (Libri3Mix) with 18.4M trainable parameters and everything else frozen. — [arXiv 2407.09817](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.09817)
- **After SOT fine-tuning the picture inverts** [paper]: with an SOT-trained WavLM-Large, feeding layer 12 / 18 / 24 features gives 31.0 / 15.2 / 7.9 (noisy 2Mix) and 19.0 / 7.3 / 3.4 (clean 2Mix); truncating the encoder to 6 / 12 / 18 / 24 layers for LLM-SOT gives 41.2 / 34.2 / 15.5 / 11.3 (noisy 2mix eval). A frozen SOT-trained encoder supports a per-talker CTC read-out at 10.1 (noisy) / 4.6 (clean) 2Mix. — [arXiv 2603.27205](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27205), [arXiv 2603.10587](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10587v1)
- **Frozen SSL features + trainable Conformer** [paper]: with WavLM-Large fully frozen as a feature extractor and a trainable Conformer encoder on top, SOT reaches 7.0 (clean) / 17.1 (noisy) Libri2Mix eval; the same paper's plain CTC-attention hybrid without a separator degrades to 10.3 / 22.0, which the authors take as evidence that "the encoder's implicit separation performed poorly". — [arXiv 2409.00815](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.00815)
- **CTC shapes how an SOT encoder lays out speakers** [paper]: visualisation shows CTC makes the encoder place different speakers in distinct temporal regions; CTC-only decoding of an SOT+CTC model gives 12.0 vs 8.8 joint (LibriSpeechMix-2mix). — [arXiv 2409.12388](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12388)
- **Single-speaker bias of ASR encoders** [paper]: single-speaker streaming ASR encoders "prioritize a specific speaker—often the one closest to the microphone or the first to appear—while disregarding other speakers… encoder states being optimized to maximize accuracy for a particular speaker". — [arXiv 2506.22646](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.22646)
- **Whisper encoder is not interference-invariant (Whisper-AT, arXiv 2307.03183)** [search-extract]: Whisper's "audio representation is actually not noise-invariant, but is instead highly correlated to non-speech sounds" — shown for background sounds, not for a competing talker. — [arXiv 2307.03183](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03183)
- **Direct probing studies**: none found. Searches for layer-wise probing of Whisper/WavLM/HuBERT on overlapped speech returned only overlap-*detection* work and probing of unrelated attributes. — e.g. [Lebourdais et al., Interspeech 2022](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2022/lebourdais22_interspeech.pdf)

### Inferences
- [inference] How the literature distinguishes "decoder hasn't learned two sentences" from "encoder lost speaker 2": (a) **freeze ablation** — train only projector + LLM-LoRA on a frozen encoder vs also training the encoder (Tencent: 46.7 vs 10.2 ⇒ encoder side for un-adapted WavLM; MT-LLM: 6.6 with frozen Whisper ⇒ not encoder-limited to the same degree); (b) **encoder-only read-out** — freeze the encoder, train a small separator + per-talker CTC, compare its WER with the LLM path (Shi: read-out ≈ LLM for 2 talkers, ≪ LLM for 3 talkers ⇒ decoder/interface side for 3 talkers); (c) **tap-depth ablation** — attach the same read-out/separator at several layers (Sidecar: information present at layer 2, gone by layer 9–12 in a frozen single-talker model).
- [inference] The Tencent (frozen WavLM fails) and MT-LLM (frozen Whisper works) results are not contradictory once the differences are listed: fully-overlapped noisy Libri2Mix vs partially-overlapped clean LibriSpeechMix; 270 h vs much more training data; WavLM last layer vs Whisper-large-v2 last layer; 7B LoRA r=32 with 76.6M trainable adapter params. None of these factors was isolated in one study.
- [inference] For Qwen3-ASR-0.6B the published evidence predicts neither outcome with confidence: its encoder is a supervised ASR encoder (like Whisper/wav2vec2-CTC, where upper layers tend to drop the non-dominant speaker), but large supervised encoders have also been shown to work frozen (MT-LLM, Sidecar-Whisper via early layers). The discriminating measurements are the three listed above, plus a read-out placed *after* the projector to test the projector separately (Shi et al. deliberately tap before the last downsampling stage because it "applies excessive downsampling").
- [inference] If the top layer of the frozen encoder turns out to be deficient but early layers are not, the literature offers two remedies: train the encoder (LoRA/full) with SOT so the upper layers learn to keep both talkers (Shi, Tencent), or split in the early layers (Sidecar) — the latter costs N passes.

### Gaps
- No direct layer-wise probing of second-speaker lexical content in Whisper / WavLM / HuBERT / Qwen audio encoders found; apparent literature gap.
- No three-way encoder-frozen vs encoder-LoRA vs full-fine-tune ablation for a multi-talker Speech-LLM found; no multi-talker ablation at all on Qwen3-ASR or an AuT-style encoder within this sub-topic.
- MT-LLM did not report an unfrozen-encoder variant; Tencent did not report a Whisper-encoder LLM variant.

## KQ5. Auxiliary training methods with zero inference cost (heads dropped at inference)

### Takeaway
They exist, but the measured benefit depends on the decoder: with an attention (AED) decoder, per-talker CTC on a separator or speaker-aware CTC gives modest gains (0–10% relative for 2 talkers, larger for noisy/3-talker); with an **LLM decoder**, jointly training the CTC branch made the SOT path **worse** in every 2-talker row of Shi et al.'s Table I. The safest zero-cost recipe in the literature is staged: adapt the encoder with SOT first, then freeze it.

### Cited Findings
- **EncSep (AED decoder; separator dropped at inference)** [paper]: eval WER vs SOT — noisy Libri2Mix 17.1 → 15.9; noisy Libri3Mix 28.2 → 26.5; clean Libri2Mix 7.0 → 7.3 (**no gain**); clean Libri3Mix 14.7 → 13.4. Plain hybrid CTC on the serialized label without separator (SOT-H) is harmful: 22.0 noisy / 10.3 clean. — [arXiv 2409.00815](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.00815)
- **SOT-CTC with an LLM decoder (heads unused at inference)** [paper]: stage-2 vs stage-1 eval WER — 1B: noisy 2Mix 11.3 → 13.4, clean 2Mix 4.6 → 5.5, noisy 3Mix 39.1 → 38.0, clean 3Mix 21.6 → 24.6; 3B: 9.8 → 11.1, 4.1 → 4.7, 31.7 → 30.7, 22.0 → 23.4; 8B: 14.3 → 17.4, 6.5 → 7.6, 47.5 → 45.5, 32.0 → 30.3. Single-stage joint training (ID-0, 1B): 11.8 / 4.0 / 33.9 / 23.7. Authors attribute the degradation to CTC blanks making speech embeddings sparse for the CNN downsamplers. — [arXiv 2509.04488](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04488)
- **SOT + CTC / SACTC auxiliary (single-stream, AED)** [paper]: LibriSpeechMix-2mix test 9.2 (SOT) → 8.8 (SOT+CTC) → 8.0 (SOT+SACTC); single-talker LibriSpeech test 4.6 → 4.5 → 4.1; unseen 3-speaker 25.3 → 23.6 → 22.6. — [arXiv 2409.12388](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12388)
- **Encoder pre-adaptation then freeze (training-schedule method)** [paper]: 10.2 → 9.0 test WER on noisy Libri2Mix when the LLM system starts from an AED-SOT-fine-tuned encoder and keeps it frozen. — [arXiv 2408.17431](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.17431)
- **Separator + CTC trained on a frozen encoder** (leaves the SOT path untouched; heads removable) [paper]: used as initialisation in 2603.27205; "The CTC heads are used only during training for the LLM-decoding systems and are removed for deployment". — [arXiv 2603.27205](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27205)
- **Target-talker auxiliary task** [paper]: adding the Target Talker Identifier loss improved *multi-talker* WER of Whisper-Sidecar (large: 4.98 → 4.66 Libri2Mix; 17.55 → 16.79 Libri3Mix) — "learning to distinguish the target talker can benefit the Sidecar's ability to separate embeddings". — [arXiv 2407.09817](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.09817)
- **Multi-task instruction training** [paper]: MT-LLM multi-task (target-talker, attribute-based ASR) training improved multi-talker WER 5.5 → 5.2 (2-spk), 10.7 → 10.2 (3-spk) at no inference cost. — [arXiv 2409.08596](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08596)
- **LLM as training-time teacher, dropped at inference** [paper]: encoder-only serialized CTC fails to train with CTC loss alone but succeeds when an adapted LLM-SOT loss is kept as teacher (see KQ1 follow-up B). — [arXiv 2603.10587](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10587v1)

### Inferences
- [inference] For a 2-talker Speech-LLM, no paper shows a reliable WER gain from a drop-at-inference per-talker CTC head; the one direct test (Shi Table I) shows a loss when the CTC gradient reaches the shared encoder/downsampler. If such a head is added, the evidence favours either (a) attaching it to a frozen encoder purely as a diagnostic/initialisation, or (b) tapping it *before* the projector's last compression stage and keeping its loss weight small (code default: CTC weight 0.3).
- [inference] Training-time cost matters: a CTC head over the LLM vocabulary is ≈131M parameters per talker in Shi's setup (262.9M for two); a small character/BPE CTC vocabulary (EncSep used 32 characters) is far cheaper but cannot be re-used as an SOP-style text prompt.

### Gaps
- No ablation found isolating a zero-inference-cost auxiliary loss for a two-talker Speech-LLM with a frozen or LoRA-only encoder.

## KQ6. How a separator / per-speaker CTC attaches to an encoder → projector → LLM pipeline (what is fed to the LLM)

### Takeaway
Published options: (A) original mixed features only, separator+CTC as loss; (B) **CTC text embeddings + mixed features** (SOP); (C) separated streams concatenated in time as a prefix (replacing or appended to the mixed prefix); (D) mixed prefix + per-layer gated cross-attention to the concatenated separated streams; (E) in-encoder split with one pass per talker. The mixed-speech prefix turned out to be indispensable in every ablation that removed it, and no paper sums streams (the one aggregation ablation found favours concatenation).

### Cited Findings
- **(A)** `[H_p; E_t]`, separator/CTC train-time only: EncSep; Shi stage-2 "SOT-CTC". — [arXiv 2409.00815](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.00815), [arXiv 2509.04488](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04488)
- **(B) SOP** `[E_sop; H_p; E_t]`: separator on the 2nd-CNN-layer output (4× downsampled encoder states), per-talker linear + greedy CTC over the LLM vocabulary, hypotheses concatenated in talker order and embedded by the LLM embedding layer; a new LoRA is the only thing trained in this stage. Removing `H_p` (text-only prompt) collapses: 34.4 / 83.2 / 19.0 / 76.5 (1B eval), worse still for 3B/8B. — [arXiv 2509.04488](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04488), [ctc_prompt.py](https://github.com/hshi-speech/Multi-talker-ASR-with-LLMs/blob/d955266085e130658007a747df58dff904a35c27/models/ctc_prompt.py)
- **(C) Separated features as prefix**: GEncSep feeds `Concat(H_sep^1..H_sep^S)` to an AED decoder's cross-attention; with an LLM, "AcousticPrompt" `[E_inst, E_aco]` gives 9.5 / 31.3 / 3.5 / 17.1 and "matched tensor" `[E_inst, H_p, E_aco]` 8.8 / 31.9 / 3.5 / 18.2 vs LLM-SOT 9.1 / 32.3 / 3.7 / 19.1 (1B-Instruct) — small or no gain. — [arXiv 2603.27205](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27205), [arXiv 2409.00815](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.00815)
- **(D) Persistent cross-attention memory**: separated streams concatenated in onset order at native 50 Hz (`T_m = K·T_e`); one single-head cross-attention adapter (bottleneck 512) per LLM layer between self-attention and FFN, residual scaled by a learned per-layer gate `g = σ(γ)`, γ initialised −2; mixed prefix kept. 7.6 / 17.4 / 3.2 / 14.1 (1B-Instruct); without the gate ("stacked") 11.1 / 21.0 / 5.5 / 15.0; with mixture instead of separated memory 8.1 / 25.6 / 3.3 / 14.8; without the mixed prefix 23.4 / 31.0 / 16.3 / 25.4. — [arXiv 2603.27205](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27205)
- **(E) In-encoder split, per-talker passes**: Sidecar masks after encoder block 2; remaining frozen encoder blocks and decoder run on each branch. — [arXiv 2407.09817](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.09817), [arXiv 2302.09908](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.09908)
- **(F) Two encoders, no separator**: frozen Whisper (semantic) + frozen WavLM (multi-layer weighted sum), adapter outputs fused and projected to the LLM. — [arXiv 2409.08596](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08596)
- **(G) Per-speaker encoder passes + one SOT decoder**: SA-DiCoW; concatenation of per-speaker embeddings beats weighted sum / average / masked average (3.9 vs 4.8 / 4.6 / 4.6 on Libri2Mix; 21.0 vs 59.1 / 50.2 / 47.4 on NOTSOFAR). — [arXiv 2510.03723](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03723)
- **(H) Per-speaker instances conditioned on activity**: NVIDIA SSA; authors list "multi-modal large language models (LLMs)" as future work. — [arXiv 2506.22646](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.22646)

### Inferences
- [inference] Mapping to Qwen3-ASR (audio encoder → projector → Qwen3 LLM): Shi's stack (WavLM → conv downsamplers + linear projector → LLaMA with LoRA) is structurally equivalent, so A–D transfer directly. The separator would tap the audio-encoder output (or an intermediate projector stage, not the most compressed one); CTC heads would use the Qwen tokenizer if SOP is intended; the original projector output must stay in the LLM input. This mapping is my inference — none of these papers used a Qwen model.
- [inference] Risk/benefit ordering for a LoRA-only 2-talker project, from the 2-talker numbers above: A changes nothing at inference but showed no gain (and a loss if CTC gradients reach shared layers); B gives ≈0.7 abs. WER (9.1 → 8.4 noisy, 3.7 → 3.1 clean); D gives the most (→ 7.6 / 3.2) at +64M parameters and ≈+55% latency; C gives ≈0; E is incompatible with single-pass SOT output.
- [inference] Most of the reported benefit of B–D is in 3-talker conditions; for exactly two talkers the absolute head-room over a well-trained LLM-SOT baseline in these papers is 1–1.5 WER points.

### Gaps
- No paper evaluates these attachment options on Mandarin or multilingual overlapped speech with an LLM decoder, on real (non-LibriMix) fully-overlapped data, or on Qwen3-ASR.
- No evidence on a separator placed after the projector, or on summing separated streams before an LLM.
